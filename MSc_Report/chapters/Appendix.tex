\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{plots/real/exp2/exp2_forecast_correction_rmse_bar.pdf}
    \caption{Whether the freely observable state of a checkpoint can stand in
    for the fan-out a refit would need, scored on $\Delta P_0$. Each bar is the
    mean of 42 checkpoint-level RMSEs spanning two traits, three trunks, and
    seven checkpoints, with each constituent RMSE calculated over eight probe
    datasets. Error bars show one sample standard deviation across those 42
    values and are descriptive rather than inferential. Bars are ordered from
    smaller to larger error, the best non-refit method is bold and orange, and
    the grey $f_t$ refit is the upper-bound reference. Here $f_0$ denotes the affine
    forecaster fitted at the initial checkpoint, and $g(\cdot)$ rescales its
    $\Delta P_0$ input. The projection is the rung
    nothing at $\mathcal{M}_t$ has refreshed, and so the one with something to
    correct.
    Each correction rescales the stale projection by a gain regressed on the
    checkpoint state: $g(\mathbf{z}_t^{[0,0]})$ on the latent state fitted
    whole, then each of its four coordinates on its own, and $g(b_t)$ on the
    behaviour level. Both response sets behind the state are $\mathcal{M}_0$'s,
    which is what the $[0,0]$ marks. The
    single-coordinate rows carry two parameters apiece, the same as $g(b_t)$,
    so a correction that fails with them cannot be failing for want of
    parsimony --- $g(\mathbf{z}_t^{[0,0]})$ spends five parameters on a fold of roughly fourteen
    checkpoints drawn from two trunks. All rows are reported at every
    checkpoint rather than only where they win. Everything here is fitted under
    one rule: nothing fitted on dataset $j$ is used to predict dataset $j$. The
    gain that corrects probe $j$ on a given trunk comes from the other trunks'
    checkpoints, whose own ideal gains were solved over the remaining probes
    and against the base line fitted without $j$. Each cell is therefore a
    cross-validated error over eight predictions made by eight different gain
    models. Those eight are not near-copies of one another: a probe enters the
    ideal gain weighted by $\Delta P_0^2$, so the one probe whose projection
    difference is several times the rest carries most of it, and the model that
    has to predict that probe is the one fitted without it. Every correction is
    as cheap to read as the others and far cheaper than re-running the probes.
    The refit bounds what any of them could achieve.}
    \label{fig:forecast-correction-rmse}
\end{figure}
